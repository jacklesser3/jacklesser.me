Philosopher-Builder Pilot

AI that strengthens judgment and preserves the ability to disagree

Decision requested

Approve a four-week scoping sprint and identify a Cosmos collaborator to review the question and shortlist. The sprint ends with a go / revise / stop recommendation for a focused workshop or project review; it does not commit funding or launch a new program.

Research question

Which design choices help people use AI to examine evidence and revise beliefs without losing the ability to question, reject or act independently of its advice? Begin with AI-assisted evaluation of conflicting claims, rather than attempting to measure autonomy across every use of AI.

Rationale & challenge

Cosmos’s public work connects self-direction with the practice of judgment, and its grants support autonomy and truth-seeking. This pilot asks how those commitments translate into observable product behavior. Challenge: more friction or disagreement does not necessarily mean more autonomy. Skilled delegation can be a deliberate choice; a tool that demands constant deliberation may burden users without improving judgment.

Selection criteria

Review each person or project against the criteria below as evidenced, unclear or not met, citing a dated primary artifact. Keep unknowns visible; reputation and follower counts do not substitute for evidence.

CriterionEvidence to look for
Question fitWork addresses evidence evaluation, AI deference, contestability or independent judgment.
Substantive contributionA paper, repository, prototype or documented study demonstrates relevant reasoning or execution.
Testable mechanismA specific design choice and a plausible way to observe its benefits, tradeoffs and failure cases.
Intellectual opennessEngagement with counterarguments, uncertainty or limitations; include a serious critic of the premise.
Useful collaborationA concrete contribution and reason Cosmos could help. Confirm interest, availability and overlap with existing work before inviting.

Plan & deliverables

Week 1: refine the question and map existing Cosmos work to avoid duplication. Week 2: prepare an evidence-linked longlist and justified shortlist with complementary research, building and critical perspectives. Week 3: review with a Cosmos collaborator; draft outreach and a pilot agenda. Week 4: deliver a decision memo, shortlist, resource estimate, proposed owners and evaluation plan. Outreach begins only after approval.

What would count as progress?

For scoping: a reviewed map, justified shortlist and explicit decision. For an approved pilot: log invitations, acceptances, attendance and agreed collaborations with denominators. Explore a comparison of unaided and AI-assisted claim evaluation, including erroneous AI advice: evidence checking, justified acceptance or rejection, and later unaided reasoning. These are proposed indicators, not a validated autonomy score or proof of lasting benefit.
