Audiobook — A Timeless Reflection

Make Your Bed

Admiral William H. McRaven — Little Things That Can Change Your Life
10 lessons Six months of SEAL training One speech that became a book

In 2014, Admiral McRaven stood before the graduating class at the University of Texas and made a strange promise: if you want to change the world, start off by making your bed.

The speech drew on the ten hardest lessons of Navy SEAL training. It became one of the most-watched commencement addresses ever given — and then a small book with a big argument: it’s the little things that change everything.

University of Texas at Austin — Commencement, 2014
I

The Foundations

01
Start your day with a task completed

Square corners, tight blanket. The first task of the day, done right — one small pride, momentum before the world gets a vote. And if the day goes badly, you come home to a bed that is made.

02
Find someone to help you paddle

No one gets through the pounding surf alone — not the strongest, not the most stubborn. Everyone paddles in rhythm, everyone pulls their weight. Be someone worth having in the boat.

03
Measure a person by the size of their heart

The “munchkin crew” — nobody over five-foot-five, little flippers, mocked by everyone — outpaddled every other boat. Nothing mattered but the will to succeed.

If you can’t do the little things right, you will never do the big things right. Admiral William H. McRaven
II

Life’s Not Fair — Drive On

04
Get over being a sugar cookie

Fail a uniform inspection — sometimes for no reason at all — and you ran into the surf, then rolled in the sand until every inch of you was covered. Sometimes you do everything right and still end up a sugar cookie. Keep moving forward.

05
Don’t be afraid of the circus

Fall short on any event and you earned two extra hours of calisthenics designed to break you. But the circuses had a side effect no one advertised: the pairs that failed the most were getting stronger.

Life is filled with circuses. You will fail. You will likely fail often. It will be painful. But if you persevere, it will make you stronger. Admiral William H. McRaven
III

Dare Greatly

06
Slide down the obstacle head first

The obstacle course record stood for years — until one student went down the “slide for life” head first. The safe way was proven. It was also the reason the record never moved.

07
Don’t back down from the sharks

Night swim off San Clemente, water thick with sharks: stand your ground, don’t act afraid — and if one charges, punch it in the snout. Bullies are all the same. They feed on fear, and they retreat the moment you show you won’t be moved.

If you hope to complete the swim, you will have to deal with the sharks. Admiral William H. McRaven
IV

The Darkest Hours

08
Be your very best in the darkest moment

Under an enemy hull at night, no light reaches you — and that’s exactly where the mission gets done. Everyone hits a keel moment eventually. What you practice in small dark moments shows up in the big one.

09
Start singing when you’re up to your neck in mud

Hell Week, freezing mud, eight hours to dawn, and a standing offer: five quitters and everyone warms up by the fire. Then one voice started singing — and the whole class believed again. Hope is contagious, and it only takes one carrier.

10
Never, ever ring the bell

Ring the brass bell in the courtyard and the suffering ends instantly — all you have to do is quit, publicly. The ones who made it weren’t the strongest. They simply refused to touch the bell. Decide in advance that quitting isn’t an option.

If you want to change the world, don’t ever, ever ring the bell. Admiral William H. McRaven
